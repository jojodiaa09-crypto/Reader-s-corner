Title: The Rosie Project

Author: Graeme Simsion

Genre: Romance and contemporary fiction

Publication year: 2013

  1. About the Book: The Rosie Project is a romantic comedy novel written by Australian author Graeme Simsion and published in 2013. The story follows an intelligent university professor who approaches life through logic, routines, and carefully planned decisions. When he begins an unexpected search for a wife, he discovers that relationships and human emotions are far more complicated than he imagined. The novel combines humor, romance, and personal growth while exploring the importance of accepting differences.
  2. Main Characters
    • Don Tillman: Don Tillman is a genetics professor who is highly intelligent, organized, and logical. He prefers routines and structured plans, especially when dealing with everyday situations and social interactions. Although he finds relationships difficult to navigate, his journey helps him reconsider his assumptions about love and compatibility.
    • Rosie Jarman: Rosie Jarman is an independent, determined, and spontaneous woman who approaches life differently from Don. She challenges his carefully organized way of thinking and encourages him to consider perspectives he might otherwise overlook. Her personality plays an important role in his personal development.
    • Gene Tillman: Gene is Don's friend and colleague. He helps Don with his search for a suitable partner and offers a contrasting perspective on relationships. His involvement also contributes to some of the novel's humorous situations.
  3. The Story: The novel follows Don Tillman, a professor who decides to find a wife through a carefully designed questionnaire intended to identify the most compatible partner. He believes that a systematic approach will help him avoid the uncertainty of dating. However, his plans take an unexpected turn when he meets Rosie Jarman, a woman who does not fit his criteria but captures his interest. As they work together on a personal project, Don begins to question his assumptions about compatibility, relationships, and the role of emotions in decision-making.
  4. Main Themes
    • Love and Compatibility: The novel explores whether love can be predicted through logic and carefully selected criteria. Don's experiences suggest that genuine compatibility may involve qualities that cannot easily be measured or organized into a checklist
    • Accepting Differences: Don and Rosie have very different personalities and ways of approaching life. Their relationship demonstrates how understanding and respecting differences can help people form meaningful connections.
    • Personal Growth: Throughout the story, Don gradually becomes more open to unfamiliar experiences. His journey highlights how relationships can challenge existing beliefs and encourage people to grow.
    • Logic and Emotions: One of the central themes is the contrast between rational thinking and emotional understanding. The novel uses humor to explore how both can influence decisions and relationships.
    • Identity and Social Expectations: The story also examines the expectations people face when it comes to dating, marriage, and fitting into society. It encourages readers to consider whether there is one correct way to live or build relationships.
  5. My Review: The Rosie Project is an entertaining and thoughtful novel that combines humor with a meaningful exploration of relationships and personal growth. One of its most interesting features is Don Tillman's unusual approach to everyday life. His attempts to apply logic to complicated social situations create amusing moments while revealing how differently people can experience the world. I particularly like the contrast between Don and Rosie. Their different personalities make their interactions engaging, and their relationship challenges the idea that people must be similar in every way to understand one another. Another strength of the novel is its balance between comedy and deeper themes. Beneath the humorous situations, it encourages readers to question their assumptions about love, compatibility, and what makes a relationship successful. Some readers may find Don's behavior frustrating at times, especially when his strict routines make communication difficult. However, this also makes his development throughout the story more noticeable. Overall, I would recommend The Rosie Project to readers who enjoy romantic comedies, unusual characters, witty storytelling, and stories about learning to understand people who see the world differently. Title:We Used to Live Here

Author:Marcus Kliewer

Genre:Psychological horror, supernatural horror, and thriller

Publication year:2024

  1. About the Book: We Used to Live Here is a psychological horror novel written by Canadian author Marcus Kliewer and published in 2024. The story explores the terrifying consequences of allowing strangers into a place that is supposed to feel safe. Through an unsettling atmosphere, mysterious events, and unexpected twists, the novel challenges readers to question their understanding of reality and the reliability of their own perceptions. It combines supernatural mystery with psychological suspense, creating a story in which an ordinary home gradually becomes a place of fear and uncertainty.
  2. Main Characters
    • Eve Palmer: Eve is a young woman who renovates houses with her partner, Charlie. She is cautious but also struggles with the desire to be polite and avoid disappointing others. When she allows a strange family into their new home, she becomes increasingly disturbed by the events that follow. Her experiences force her to question what she sees, what she remembers, and whether she can trust her own judgment.
    • Charlie: Charlie is Eve's partner, and the two of them have recently purchased an old house to renovate. She is more direct than Eve and often approaches situations differently. As the strange events surrounding the house intensify, Charlie's presence and behavior become increasingly important to the mystery.
    • Thomas Faust: Thomas is a man who arrives unexpectedly at Eve and Charlie's home with his family. He claims that he lived in the house years earlier and asks to show his children around. Although his request initially seems harmless, his visit marks the beginning of a series of disturbing events.
    • The Faust Family: Thomas's wife, Paige, and their three children accompany him to the house. Their presence gradually makes Eve uncomfortable, and their unusual behavior raises questions about their true intentions and their connection to the property.
  3. The Story: The novel follows Eve Palmer and her partner, Charlie, who purchase an old house in a quiet, picturesque area with plans to renovate it. Their lives take an unexpected turn when a man named Thomas arrives at their door with his family, explaining that he used to live there and would like to show his children around. Although Eve feels uncertain about the visitors, she eventually allows them inside. What begins as an ordinary visit soon becomes deeply unsettling as strange and inexplicable events begin to occur. The family does not seem willing to leave, one of the children disappears, and the house appears to hold secrets that neither Eve nor Charlie understands. As the situation becomes more frightening, Charlie suddenly vanishes, and Eve begins to question her perception of reality. She struggles to determine whether the danger comes from the house, the visiting family, or something she cannot fully understand. The story gradually builds suspense through its mysteries and unexpected developments, leaving readers uncertain about what is real and what might be imagined.
  4. Main Themes
    • Fear of the Unknown: One of the central themes is the fear that arises when familiar surroundings become unfamiliar. The house initially represents a new beginning for Eve and Charlie, but it gradually becomes a source of uncertainty and danger.
    • Trust and Boundaries: Eve's decision to welcome strangers into her home highlights the difficulty of balancing politeness with personal safety. The story explores how trusting others without fully understanding their intentions can lead to uncomfortable and frightening situations.
    • Reality and Perception: As strange events unfold, Eve struggles to distinguish between what is actually happening and what she might be imagining. This creates psychological tension and encourages readers to question how much they can trust their own perceptions.
    • The Meaning of Home: A home is usually associated with comfort, security, and belonging. In this novel, however, the house becomes a place of fear and uncertainty. This contrast demonstrates how easily a familiar environment can lose its sense of safety.
    • Memory and the Past: The visitors' claim that they once lived in the house raises questions about its history and the influence of past events on the present. The story suggests that places can hold mysteries that are not immediately visible to the people living in them.
  5. My Review: We Used to Live Here is a suspenseful and unsettling novel that combines psychological horror with supernatural mystery. One of its strongest aspects is its ability to transform an ordinary situation into something deeply disturbing. The idea of strangers arriving at someone's home with a seemingly reasonable request creates an intriguing starting point for the story. I particularly appreciate how the author builds suspense gradually rather than explaining everything immediately. Each unusual event introduces new questions, encouraging readers to continue reading in search of answers. Eve is also an interesting character because her uncertainty makes the story more psychologically intense. As she struggles to understand what is happening, readers are encouraged to question their own interpretations of the events. Another strength of the novel is its use of the house as more than just a setting. It becomes an important part of the mystery and symbolizes the uncertainty surrounding the characters' lives. However, some readers may find the story confusing because it leaves certain questions unanswered and deliberately blurs the boundary between reality and the supernatural. Overall, I would recommend this book to readers who enjoy psychological horror, mysterious settings, and stories filled with suspense and unexpected twists. It is particularly suitable for readers who appreciate novels that keep them guessing and leave them thinking about the story even after finishing the final page.
  6. My Favorite Quotes

    • “Even when the event was completely out of her control, even when the other party was blatantly overstepping her bounds, she always found a way to blame herself. Always felt this nagging sense of guilt for everything, as if her very existence was a violation of some stone-etched decree.”

My final thoughts: We Used to Live Here is a disturbing and thought-provoking story about fear, trust, memory, and the uncertainty of reality. Through its mysterious characters and increasingly unsettling events, Marcus Kliewer transforms a familiar setting into the center of a psychological nightmare. The novel's greatest strength lies in its ability to create suspense without immediately providing clear answers. Although its ambiguous elements may not appeal to everyone, it offers a memorable reading experience for fans of horror and mystery. Ultimately, the story encourages readers to reconsider what makes a place feel like home and how easily that sense of security can be challenged.

Title: Me Before You

Author: Jojo Moyes

Genre: Romance and contemporary fiction

Publication year: 2012

  1. About the Book: Me Before You is a contemporary romance novel written by British author Jojo Moyes and published in 2012. The story explores how unexpected relationships can change people's lives and challenge the way they think about happiness, independence, and the future. Through its emotional storytelling and complex characters, the novel encourages readers to reflect on love, personal choices, and the meaning of living a fulfilling life.
  2. Main Characters
    • louisa Clark: Louisa Clark is a cheerful, kind-hearted young woman who lives a simple life in a small English town. She is creative, talkative, and full of personality, but she has not explored many opportunities beyond her familiar surroundings. As the story develops, she begins to discover new perspectives and possibilities.
    • Will Traynor: Will Traynor is an intelligent, confident, and successful man whose life changes dramatically after an accident. Before the accident, he enjoyed an active lifestyle filled with adventure and exciting experiences. Afterwards, he struggles to adjust to his new circumstances, and his experiences influence the way he views life and future.
  3. The Story: The novel follows Louisa Clark, who loses her job at a local café and takes a position caring for Will Traynor, a wealthy man whose life has changed following a serious accident. At first, the two have very different personalities and find it difficult to understand each other. However, as they spend more time together, their relationship begins to develop in unexpected ways. Through their conversations and shared experiences, both characters encounter new challenges and perspectives that affect their understanding of life, happiness, and personal freedom.
  4. Main Themes
    • Love and Relationships: The novel explores how relationships can influence our lives and help us understand perspectives different from our own. Louisa and Will's connection develops gradually, showing the importance of communication, empathy, and companionship.
    • Personal Choices: One of the central themes is the difficulty of making deeply personal decisions. The story encourages readers to consider how individual experiences, values, and circumstances can shape the choices people make.
    • Independence and Personal Growth: Louisa's journey highlights the importance of stepping outside one's comfort zone and discovering new opportunities. Her experiences encourage her to reconsider her limitations and think more seriously about her future.
    • Changing Perspectives: As the characters get to know each other, they begin to see the world differently. Their experiences demonstrate how meeting someone with a different background can challenge our assumptions and encourage personal reflection.
  5. My Review: Me Before You is an emotional and thought-provoking novel that combines romance with deeper questions about life and personal freedom. One of its strongest aspects is the contrast between Louisa and Will. Their different personalities make their conversations interesting, and their relationship gives the story much of its emotional impact. I particularly appreciate how the characters develop throughout the novel. Louisa's journey is especially interesting because she gradually begins to recognize that there is more to life than the familiar world she has always known. The story also encourages readers to think beyond simple answers and consider complicated situations from different perspectives. However, some parts of the novel may feel emotionally heavy, and its difficult themes may not appeal to every reader. Overall, I would recommend this book to readers who enjoyc emotional relationships, and novels that encourage reflection long after the final page.
  6. My Favorite Quotes
    • Will expresses his deep affection for Louisa: "You are scored on my heart, Clark...",
    • Will notes, "I will never, ever regret the things I've done. Because most days, all you have are places in your memory that you can go to."
    • Will expresses his affection for Louisa:"Sometimes, Clark, you are pretty much the only thing that makes me want to get up in the morning."
    • Louisa likewise shares her profound commitment: "All I can say is that you make me… into someone I couldn't even imagine."
My final thoughts: Me Before You is a story about relationships, change, and the complicated decisions people face. Its memorable characters and emotional themes make it a novel worth discussing, especially for readers who enjoy stories that explore more than just romance.

Title: A Tale of Two Cities

Author: Charles Dickens

Genre: Historical fiction, historical drama, and classic literature

Publication year: 1859

  1. About the Book: A Tale of Two Cities is a historical novel written by English author Charles Dickens and published in 1859. Set in London and Paris during the years leading up to and during the French Revolution, the novel explores the effects of social inequality, political oppression, and violence on individuals and society. Through the lives of its characters, Dickens examines the complicated relationship between justice and revenge, as well as the power of love, sacrifice, and personal transformation. The novel is widely recognized for its memorable characters, dramatic storytelling, and exploration of the human consequences of historical events.
  2. Main Characters
    • Lucie Manette: Lucie is a kind-hearted and compassionate young woman who is reunited with her father after he spends eighteen years imprisoned in the Bastille. She represents love, hope, and emotional strength. Her caring nature helps her father recover from his traumatic experiences and brings comfort to the people around her.
    • Charles Darnay: Charles is a French aristocrat who rejects the cruelty and privileges associated with his family's background. He chooses to live in England and attempts to build an honest life. However, his family history continues to affect his future, especially when the French Revolution places him in danger.
    • Sydney Carton: Sydney is an intelligent English lawyer who struggles with disappointment and a lack of confidence in his own abilities. Although he initially appears careless and dissatisfied with his life, he possesses a deeply compassionate nature. His relationship with Lucie eventually inspires him to make a decision that demonstrates the possibility of personal redemption.
    • Dr. Alexandre Manette: Dr. Manette is Lucie's father, a French physician who has been imprisoned in the Bastille for eighteen years. His experiences leave him emotionally vulnerable, and he struggles to rebuild his life after his release. Through his relationship with Lucie, he gradually begins to recover and find a sense of security.
    • Madame Defarge: Madame Defarge is a determined revolutionary who seeks revenge against the aristocratic family responsible for suffering in her own family. Although her desire for justice is connected to genuine injustice, her growing hatred makes her increasingly ruthless. Her character illustrates how suffering and revenge can lead to further violence.
  3. The Story: The novel begins in 1775 and follows events in London and Paris during a period of significant political and social change. Jarvis Lorry, a banker, travels to France to help Lucie Manette reunite with her father, Dr. Alexandre Manette, who has recently been released after eighteen years of imprisonment in the Bastille. Together, Lucie and her father return to England, where they attempt to rebuild their lives. Several years later, Lucie becomes connected to two very different men: Charles Darnay, a Frenchman who has rejected his family's cruel aristocratic traditions, and Sydney Carton, a talented but troubled lawyer who secretly loves her. Lucie eventually marries Charles, and they establish a family life in England. Meanwhile, the French Revolution begins to transform France. The people rise against the aristocracy in response to years of oppression, poverty, and inequality. However, the revolution gradually becomes associated with widespread fear, revenge, and violence. When Charles returns to France to help someone in danger, he is arrested because of his aristocratic family background. Despite his personal rejection of his family's abuses, he becomes a target of revolutionary justice. His situation places Lucie and Dr. Manette in a desperate position as they attempt to save him. As Charles faces the possibility of execution, Sydney Carton makes a courageous decision that changes the lives of the people he loves. His final act reveals the depth of his character and becomes the novel's most powerful expression of sacrifice and redemption.
  4. Main Themes
    • Sacrifice and Redemption: One of the novel's central themes is the possibility of changing one's life through selfless actions. Sydney Carton's journey demonstrates that a person who once considered himself unsuccessful can still perform an extraordinary act of kindness and give his life greater meaning.
    • Revenge and Justice: The novel explores the difference between seeking justice and pursuing revenge. Although the French Revolution begins as a response to genuine injustice, the violence that follows demonstrates how the desire to punish the guilty can become destructive when it turns into hatred.
    • Social Inequality and Oppression: Dickens portrays the suffering of ordinary people under an unjust social system. The contrast between the privileged aristocracy and the impoverished population helps explain the anger that contributes to the French Revolution.
    • Love and Compassion: Lucie's kindness and devotion to her family demonstrate the importance of emotional connection during difficult times. Her relationships provide hope and stability in a world increasingly affected by conflict and uncertainty.
    • Transformation and Rebirth: Several characters experience significant changes throughout the novel. Dr. Manette gradually rebuilds his life after imprisonment, Charles attempts to separate himself from his family's past, and Sydney Carton discovers a greater purpose through his love for Lucie.
    • The Destructive Power of Hatred: Through the events of the Revolution and Madame Defarge's pursuit of revenge, Dickens demonstrates how hatred can continue cycles of suffering rather than bring lasting peace.
  5. My Review: A Tale of Two Cities is a powerful and thought-provoking novel that combines historical events with deeply personal stories of love, sacrifice, and redemption. One of its greatest strengths is the way Charles Dickens connects the political turmoil of the French Revolution with the experiences of ordinary individuals. Rather than presenting history only through major events, he shows how social injustice and political violence can transform people's lives. I particularly appreciate the contrast between Charles Darnay and Sydney Carton. Although the two men look remarkably alike, their personalities and life choices are very different. Their similarities and differences contribute to the novel's central questions about identity, personal responsibility, and the possibility of change. Sydney Carton is especially memorable because his character develops from someone who appears disappointed with life into someone capable of extraordinary selflessness. His transformation gives the story much of its emotional power and demonstrates that a person's worth cannot be measured only by their past mistakes. Another strength of the novel is its exploration of the French Revolution. Dickens shows that while fighting against oppression may be necessary, violence and revenge can create new forms of injustice. However, some readers may find the language challenging because of its nineteenth-century style, and the historical background may require additional context to understand fully. Overall, I would recommend this book to readers who enjoy classic literature, historical fiction, and stories that explore complex moral questions. Although it requires patience and attention, the novel offers meaningful insights into human nature, society, and the importance of compassion.
  6. My Favorite Quotes
    • "It was the best of times, it was the worst of times." This famous opening line introduces the contradictions of the historical period, when hope and progress existed alongside suffering and uncertainty.
    • "Recalled to life." This recurring phrase represents the possibility of renewal and transformation, particularly in Dr. Manette's journey from imprisonment toward a new life.
    • "I am the resurrection and the life." This biblical phrase is associated with the novel's themes of rebirth, sacrifice, and the possibility of a new beginning.
My final thoughts: A Tale of Two Cities is more than a historical novel about the French Revolution. It is a story about how people respond to injustice, how love can inspire courage, and how the desire for revenge can become destructive. Through memorable characters and dramatic events, Charles Dickens encourages readers to reflect on the importance of compassion, personal responsibility, and moral courage. Although its language and historical setting may make it challenging for modern readers, its central themes remain relevant. Ultimately, the novel reminds us that even during periods of great suffering and uncertainty, individual acts of kindness and sacrifice can have a lasting impact.
